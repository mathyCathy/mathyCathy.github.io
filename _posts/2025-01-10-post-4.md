Just reminding myself why 2x2 factorial designs give "two trials for the price of one."

Assumptions: 
* Two treatments under consideration, $A$ and $B$.
* 2x2 factorial design with $n$ subjects assigned to each treatment combination (total subjects = $4n$).
* Normally distributed outcome modeled by $y = \beta_0 + \beta_A I(A=1) + \beta_B I(B=1) + \epsilon$, where $\epsilon$ are i.i.d. $N(0,\sigma^2)$.
