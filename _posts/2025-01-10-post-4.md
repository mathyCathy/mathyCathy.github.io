Just reminding myself why 2x2 factorial designs give "two trials for the price of one."

### Assumptions: 
* Two treatments under consideration, $A$ and $B$.
* 2x2 factorial design with $n$ subjects assigned to each treatment combination (total sample size = $4n$).
* Normally distributed outcome modeled by $y = \beta_0 + \beta_A I(A=1) + \beta_B I(B=1) + \epsilon$, where $\epsilon$ are i.i.d. $N(0,\sigma^2)$. (Note: this assumes no interaction.) 

### Variance for estimate of $\beta_A$
Note that $\beta_A = 0.5*[E(y|A=1,B=0)-E(y|A=0,B=0)+E(y|A=1,B=1)-E(y|A=0,B=1)]$. Each expectation can be estimated by the mean of the outcomes ($y$) with each treatment combination, i.e., a mean of $n$ values with variance $\sigma^2/n$.
